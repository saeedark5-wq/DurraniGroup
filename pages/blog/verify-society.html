How to Verify a Housing Society Before You Invest

By Mubashir Durrani — CEO, Durrani Group of Companies • 5 min read

As CEO of a group that has delivered projects across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, I have seen the damage that unverified societies cause to families who trusted the wrong file. The good news? In 2026, verification is easier than ever — if you know what to check. Here is the exact checklist we use, and that every buyer should use.

Step 1: NOC Status — The Non-Negotiable

A valid NOC from the provincial development authority is the foundation of a legitimate society. Verify it directly with the authority or on the official portal — never accept a photocopy as proof. If a seller hesitates to let you verify, walk away. That hesitation is the most expensive signal in real estate.

Step 2: Land Ownership and Possession

Confirm the society’s ownership documents and that physical possession of the land is with the society. A society that has taken possession and begun development — roads, boundary walls, offices — is far safer than one that only exists on paper.

Step 3: The Society Office Itself

Visit the society office in person. An established office with real staff, allotment registers and payment records is the strongest sign of a functioning society. Our own projects in Malik Town and Durrani Enclave operate full offices on site for exactly this reason.

15-Minute Verification Checklist

  • NOC verified with the authority — not from a photocopy
  • Land ownership and possession documents checked
  • Physical development visible on site (roads, offices, boundary)
  • Allotment letters and receipts issued on society letterhead
  • Payment goes to the society’s bank account, not an individual
